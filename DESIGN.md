# ALTNTVE — Visual Design Reference

## Overall Style

Minimalist editorial design with a premium creative-studio aesthetic. Clean, monochromatic, spacious, and typography-led. The design feels like a photography portfolio or independent design studio publication rather than a conventional commercial brochure.

## 1. Color Palette

- **White:** Dominant background, creating an airy and uncluttered appearance.
- **Black:** Primary text, headings, package names, and prices.
- **Light gray:** Decorative illustrations, dotted maps, and thin geometric lines.

Keep the palette almost entirely monochromatic. Color comes mainly from the photography.

## 2. Typography

- Clean, modern sans-serif typography.
- Uppercase section headings with wide letter spacing.
- Bold, compact brand wordmark.
- Regular-weight body text with tighter letter spacing.
- Prices and package names use stronger font weights.
- Clear contrast between spacious headings and compact supporting text.

Typography should feel deliberate, editorial, and precise—not decorative or playful.

## 3. Signature Graphics

- **Dotted world map:** Large, pale-gray particle illustration used as a background watermark.
- **Orbital globe:** Thin intersecting circles and elliptical lines surrounding a central dotted map.
- **Plus signs:** Small, light-gray geometric ornaments, especially on the cover.
- **Oval emblem:** Minimal line-art brand symbol used on selected interior pages.

Graphics are oversized but subtle, often extending beyond the content area. They provide texture without competing with the typography.

## 4. Layout and Composition

- Portrait-oriented pages with generous white space.
- Asymmetrical editorial composition rather than rigidly centered layouts.
- Consistent left alignment for headings and body text.
- Large visual elements balanced by smaller, carefully positioned text.
- Minimal borders and almost no decorative containers.
- Compact footer branding, typically with a small emblem on the left and bold wordmark on the right.

The composition should feel spacious and intentionally arranged, never crowded.

## 5. Photography

- Large, atmospheric photographs that serve as the main visual focus.
- Editorial portraiture with natural or directional lighting.
- Strong environmental context, architectural backgrounds, and dramatic contrast.
- Full-page photographs and large image panels.
- Minimal framing, overlays, and decorative effects.

Photography provides the emotional character; the surrounding layout remains quiet and restrained.

## 6. Pricing Layout

- Package information presented in horizontal rows rather than cards.
- Package names aligned left, prices emphasized in bold, and specifications aligned consistently to the right.
- Thin black horizontal dividers between rows.
- Simple bullet lists for package inclusions.
- Pale-gray map graphics placed behind selected pricing sections.

The pricing system should feel like a clean editorial catalogue, with alignment and typography doing the work.

## 7. Information Pages

- Wide-tracked uppercase headings.
- Numbered process steps and terms presented as simple text lists.
- Short paragraphs with consistent alignment.
- Clear vertical separation between sections.
- No colorful callout boxes or excessive icons.

Information-heavy pages remain visually consistent with the more image-driven pages through typography, spacing, and restrained branding.

## 8. Cover Composition

- Large, pale-gray dotted globe with fine orbital lines.
- Central black brand wordmark.
- Document title and year near the bottom.
- Small geometric ornaments between the title and contact details.
- Location and social/contact information aligned toward opposite bottom corners.

The cover is graphic-led, spacious, and immediately recognizable.

## 9. Visual Consistency

Maintain the same monochromatic palette, typography, geometric graphics, footer treatment, and spacing principles throughout the document. Use photography and page composition to create variety without changing the core identity.

## Avoid

- Rounded pricing cards and heavy shadows.
- Bright accent colors or gradients.
- Thick borders and excessive icons.
- Overly decorative fonts.
- Dense layouts with insufficient white space.
- High-contrast background illustrations behind text.

## Core Art Direction

**Minimalist editorial × monochromatic branding × technical geometric graphics × atmospheric photography.**

The defining visual characteristic is the balance between expansive white space, widely tracked typography, subtle gray illustrations, and bold photographic imagery. Every element should feel intentional, understated, and precisely positioned.
